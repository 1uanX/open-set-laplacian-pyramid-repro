# Claims

## Empirical Claims

- Claim: The PR-RPAD method outperforms SDIF, PRI-Tran, BLSTM, BGRU, and DCN in closed-set deinterleaving under the three mixed datasets of Experiments 1-3.
  Type: empirical
  Scope: closed-set scenario only; evaluated on mixed simulated datasets with increasing difficulty.
  Source: Section V-D, Tables IX-XI, [page 12], "PR-RPAD algorithm completely outperforms the traditional algorithms..."

- Claim: The PR-RPAD method outperforms SDIF and PRI-Tran in open-set deinterleaving under Experiments 4-6.
  Type: empirical
  Scope: open-set scenario only; evaluated with `ACC_known` and `OSCR`.
  Source: Section V-D, Table XII, [page 14], "the PR-RPAD method substantially outperforms conventional algorithms like SDIF and PRI-Tran..."

- Claim: In closed-set Experiments 1-3, the training loss stabilizes around 40 iterations and the accuracy stabilizes around 60 iterations.
  Type: empirical
  Scope: mixed datasets for Experiments 1-3.
  Source: Section V-D, [page 12], "loss curve steadily declines... stabilize at roughly 40 iterations... accuracy curve tends to stabilize at roughly 60 iterations."

- Claim: In open-set Experiments 4-6, the PR-RPAD model also converges after roughly 40-60 iterations.
  Type: empirical
  Scope: mixed datasets for Experiments 4-6.
  Source: Section V-D, [pages 13-14], the discussion around Fig. 13.

- Claim: The model input used in experiments is the PDG-enhanced image with resolution `65 x 65`, generated under the `lenwindow = 1` setting.
  Type: empirical
  Scope: all experiments in the paper.
  Source: Section V-C, [page 11], "The PDGs were generated in all tests under the lenwindow = 1 condition... images with a 65 x 65 resolution."

## Conceptual Claims

- Claim: Radar signal deinterleaving can be transformed from pulse-stream processing into image-style recognition by converting PDWs into pulse description graphs (PDGs).
  Type: conceptual
  Scope: the paper's overall formulation from PDW to PDG.
  Source: Section III, [pages 4-5], "Through data imageization mapping and amplification enhancement processing, RSD is transformed into ISS."

- Claim: Symmetric gray-matrix mapping of `DTOA`, `RF`, `PW`, `PA`, and `DOA` can improve separability between radar sources and expose pulse-to-pulse positional relations.
  Type: conceptual
  Scope: the preprocessing stage that forms PDGs.
  Source: Section III-B, [pages 4-5], "This method can extend the differences... and visualizes the positional relationship between the current pulse, the preceding pulse, and the succeeding pulse."

- Claim: Otsu-threshold-based PDG amplification and enhancement improves edge and texture clarity of low-resolution PDGs.
  Type: conceptual
  Scope: the image enhancement stage before model input.
  Source: Section III-C, [pages 5-6], "extends the visual representation... and produces more stable and better-magnified images in terms of edge and texture feature retention."

- Claim: A Laplacian-pyramid reconstruction and fusion model can combine low-frequency edge information and high-frequency texture information at multiple resolutions.
  Type: conceptual
  Scope: the multiresolution feature extraction stage.
  Source: Section IV-A, [page 6], "successive reconstructions starting with coarse-scale low-frequency segmentation estimations and adding data from high-frequency subbands."

- Claim: Reciprocal points can model open space and reduce closed-set bias when distinguishing known and unknown radar radiation sources.
  Type: conceptual
  Scope: the open-set recognition component of the method.
  Source: Section IV-B, [pages 7-8], equations (3)-(11) and the surrounding text.

- Claim: Confusing samples generated by adversarial reciprocal-point learning improve the classifier's ability to reject unknown radar radiation sources.
  Type: conceptual
  Scope: the generator-discriminator-classifier training procedure in open-set mode.
  Source: Section IV-B, [pages 7-8], "an instantiated adversarial augmentation method is used to create confusing training samples..."
